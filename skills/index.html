Skill Guide: Skills, Gauntlet & Oni Awakening

Musashi grows in three separate ways at once — skills bought at shrines, a Gauntlet upgraded through a Spirit Mirror, and an Awakening system fueled by a soul type most players won't notice until it matters. Here's how they fit together.

This guide reflects what's known shortly after launch (September 4, 2026), cross-checked across multiple independent community sources. One early source described "Blazing State" as a purchasable skill and the top early pick — more detailed skill breakdowns don't list it that way, so we've left it out as a skill entry below and treated it as the combat mechanic it actually is (see our combat guide). We'd rather flag a conflict than guess.

Basic Skills vs. Special Skills

Skills are purchased at any shrine checkpoint using Red Souls, then enhanced further with one of two stone types — 54 of each are scattered across Kyoto in chests and Chance Encounter rewards (see our collectibles guide for the full 274-item breakdown these belong to, and our shrine locations guide for what a shrine/Spirit Mirror actually is).

The Gauntlet: a separate upgrade track

Interact with a Spirit Mirror and choose Enhance Equipment/Abilities to grow the Gauntlet itself — this is distinct from buying skills at a shrine. Gauntlet upgrades improve overall Oni power and Soul Absorption speed/range, and need 5 Enhancement Progress nodes per major rank. Materials include boss drops like Malicite, Byakue's Talisman, and Dohatsu-ten's Horn — see our boss guides for those fights.

Oni Awakening

A fourth Soul type — Purple Souls — fills a separate Awakening Gauge. They're generated by landing hits while an Oni Armament is active, which means the resource chain runs through your armaments rather than standing on its own:

Blue Souls feed Armaments, Armament hits feed Awakening

While Awakened, Musashi is immune to health and stamina damage — hits drain the Awakening Gauge instead — and Issen becomes noticeably easier to land, matching what our boss guides note about Yoshitsune's third phase requiring Awakening just to deal damage. You can also spend the remaining gauge on Onimusha Onslaught, a heavy finisher that staggers bosses or instantly kills lesser enemies. Save it for genuinely hard encounters rather than burning it on fights you'd win normally anyway.

Where to spend your first Souls

  • Upgrade the starting katana before spreading out. Concentrating early Souls on your main weapon outperforms thin investment across everything at once.
  • Prioritize Issen skills first, then Break Issen Combo, then Deflect — in that order, per the most consistent reporting we've found.
  • Soul Absorption Speed is a strong early quality-of-life pick — less time standing still and vulnerable while collecting Souls after a fight.
  • Don't skip health and clothing upgrades early just to chase offense — they buy you room to actually learn Issen timing without being punished for every mistake.
  • Pick up Oni Strength and Oni Agility as they become available — both gate real exploration progress, not just combat options.

Practical takeaway: spend your first Red Souls on the katana and Issen-line skills, keep an eye out for Power Stones and Oni Stones in chests rather than farming for them specifically, and treat Gauntlet upgrades at the Spirit Mirror as a separate to-do from your shrine skill shopping list — the two systems run in parallel, not in sequence.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Power Stones and Oni Stones?
Power Stones enhance Basic Skills, Oni Stones enhance Special Skills and weapon abilities — 54 of each exist in the world.

Is Blazing State a skill you buy?
No — it's a combat state from stacking Parries, not a purchase. We're flagging this because one early source described it as a skill and we don't think that holds up.

How do you trigger Oni Awakening?
Fill the Awakening Gauge with Purple Souls, generated by landing hits while an Oni Armament is active.
