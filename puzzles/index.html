Puzzle Solutions

Most puzzles in the game teach you their own logic if you look closely enough — the trick is usually reading an environmental clue correctly, not guessing. Here's every puzzle type we've confirmed, the rule behind each, and specific solutions where we have them.

This guide reflects what's known shortly after launch (September 4, 2026), cross-checked across multiple independent community sources. We have confirmed solutions for some specific puzzle instances and the general rule for solving others of the same type — we're not inventing exact answers for chests or masks we haven't verified ourselves, so some entries describe the method rather than a specific solved example.

The Mask Puzzle

Underground Laboratory, Level 3 — "What Lurks in the Shadows"

Reached during the questline of the same name, this room presents four masks you need to shoot with your bow in the correct order.

The rule: step outside the doorway and look at the shadows the masks cast on the wall. Each shadow has a number of holes letting light through — count them. Shoot the masks in order from the fewest holes to the most.

Reported solution for this specific room: 3rd mask → 4th mask → 1st mask → 2nd mask. If your room looks different, trust the hole-counting rule over this exact sequence — it's the part that's guaranteed to transfer.

Reward: the Unsettling Mask, which is needed for story progression — see our quest walkthrough for where this sits in Chapter 3.

Number Rotator chests

These locked chests are built as 3x3 magic squares — nine dials you rotate so that every row, column, and diagonal adds up to the same target sum. Different chests use different targets:

LocationTarget sum
Gion (Chapter 2)15
Crypt (Chapter 4)21
Golden Pavilion (Chapter 7)24

Confirmed solution: Gion, northern courtyard alley

Rotate the top row one step right, then the center dial two steps left, to reach the target sum of 15.

We haven't independently verified exact rotation steps for the Crypt or Golden Pavilion chests yet — if you're working on those, the magic-square logic is the same: adjust dials until every line (not just one row) sums to the target before assuming you're done.

Hidden Map treasures

Rather than a single puzzle type, this is a recurring system: find an Old Drawing somewhere in the world, and it gives you a visual clue — a landmark, a rock formation, a building silhouette — pointing toward a hidden treasure. A green search area appears on your map to narrow the general region, and matching what's in the drawing to what's actually in front of you pinpoints the exact spot.

These become available progressively as you advance through the story rather than all at once, so don't worry about ones that don't appear yet — they're tied to your current chapter, not something you can miss by moving forward normally.

Practical takeaway: for any puzzle that looks like it has a "trick," check for an environmental clue first — a shadow, a drawing, a visible pattern — before brute-forcing combinations. Onimusha's puzzles have consistently rewarded reading the room over guessing so far.

Frequently asked questions

How do you solve the mask puzzle?
Count the light-holes in each mask's shadow from outside the doorway, then shoot them in order from fewest holes to most.

What are Number Rotator chests?
3x3 magic-square puzzles — rotate dials so every row, column, and diagonal sums to the chest's target number.

How does the Hidden Map system work?
Old Drawings give a visual clue to a treasure's location, narrowed by a green search area on the map.
