Weapon Guide: Katana, Bow & Oni Armaments

There's no sword-collecting here — Musashi carries one katana for the whole game and grows it through an upgrade tree instead. Variety comes from two stances, a utility bow, and seven demon-forged Oni Armaments you commit to in bursts — one of the 11 categories in our collectibles guide.

This guide reflects what's known shortly after launch (September 4, 2026), cross-checked across multiple independent community sources. Oni Armament effects and upgrade values may be adjusted by balance patches.

The katana: one blade, two stances

Rather than swapping swords, you switch between two stances on the same katana — free to do at any time, so treat it as a habit tied to what you're fighting rather than a build lock-in.

StanceFeelBest against
One-HandedFast swings, short recovery, longer combo chainsQuick enemies, groups, Issen fishing — see our combat guide
Two-HandedSlower, more power, more reach, more staggerGuard-heavy enemies and converting boss punish windows into max damage

The katana tree improves attack power, refines the Issen counter, and also improves Oni Armament stats — upgrading your main blade isn't a separate track from your burst weapons, it feeds both.

The bow: not a damage weapon

The bow exists to solve specific problems rather than to deal your main damage:

  • Sniping distant or isolated enemies before they join a fight, thinning packs on your terms
  • Cutting tethers — some bosses rely on a physical cord or binding (revealed with Oni Vision) that shutting down disables a dangerous attack pattern entirely; see our boss guides for a specific example

Arrows are limited, so save them for these specific jobs rather than chipping general damage with them.

Oni Armaments: committed burst weapons

Fill the Oni Power Gauge with Blue Souls and you can activate an Oni Armament — doing so briefly slows time to let you reposition, then the armament stays active until the gauge fully drains. Treat this as a resource you commit to, not something you tap in and out of mid-combo.

The four Soul types

SoulWhat it's for
Red SoulsUniversal upgrade currency — katana, clothing, abilities, the gauntlet itself
Blue SoulsFuels Oni Armament activation via the Oni Power Gauge
Yellow SoulsGenerated by hits (especially Oni Armament hits); restores health
Purple SoulsGenerated by landing hits while an Oni Armament is active; fills the Oni Awakening gauge — see our skill guide

See our combat guide for how Blazing State and Break Issen tie into Soul generation more broadly.

Practical takeaway: default to One-Handed for everyday fights and swap to Two-Handed specifically for guard-heavy enemies or a boss's punish window — it costs nothing to switch. Save arrows for tether-cutting rather than chip damage, and pick an Oni Armament based on the fight in front of you (The Two Celestials as your general-purpose pick, Flashing Void against a single large target, Wind-Whipper when you're outnumbered) rather than a single favorite.

Frequently asked questions

Do you get multiple swords?
No — one katana for the whole game, deepened through an upgrade tree, with two switchable stances.

How do you activate an Oni Armament?
Fill the Oni Power Gauge with Blue Souls; activating it slows time briefly, then the armament stays active until the gauge drains.

What are the Soul types for?
Red Souls are the universal upgrade currency, Blue Souls fuel Oni Armaments, Yellow Souls (from hits) restore health, and Purple Souls (from Armament hits) fill the Oni Awakening gauge.
